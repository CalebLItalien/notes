# The Technical and Social History of Software Engineering - Capers Jones

## Chapter 1: Prelude: Computing from Ancient Times to the Modern Era
- The need to compute likely began with the need to acumulate physical possessions
- Then needed to record this information, started with physical objects and later moved to symbols
- Eventually, "the labor involved with calculations was seen as burdensome and tedious, so mechanical devices that could speed up calculations ... were developed" (ie: the abacus)

### Early Sequence of Numerical Knowledge
- Counting began as early as 35,000 years ago
- "Cave paintings date back more than 40,000 years, so at least some form of abstraction did exist [in ancient times]"
- See page 4/5 for a "probably early sequence of humans acquiring numerical knowledge"

### Inventions for Improved Mathematics
- "Calculating devices, computers, and software were all first invented to speed up mathematical calculations"
- "In the 1960s, software patents were barred ... with the courts generally concurring that software was not patentable"
- In 1981, the U.S. Supreme Court decided in Diamond vs. Diehr that in special cases, software could be patented
- In 1998, State Street Bank vs. Signature Financial Group decided that "business processes, including those embodied in software, were patentable"

## Chapter 2: 1930 to 1939: The Foundations of Digital Computing
- Original papers leading to design of digital computers in early 1930s

### The First Innovators of Modern Computing
- Anticipation of WW2 led to funding for efficient calculation devices
- Analog devices for military purposes first built/tested in this time
- In 1930 Vannevar Bush develops the differential calculator (analog)
- In 1934 Konrad Zuse suggests a "calculating engine would need a control unit, memory, and an arithmetic unit"
- In 1936 Zuse tried to build a relay-based computer Z1 but failed due to mechanical issues - did feature programmability. Later went on to make the Z2 and in 1941 the Z3
- In 1936, Alan Turing publishes "On Computable Numbers" which was "a description fo a working computer with an executable program"
- Turing machine = "abstract depiction of a working computer that sequentially processes instructions and performs mathematical and logical operations"
- In 1937 Claude Shannon (graduate student at MIT) proves that electrical relays could implement boolean logic (Victor Shestakov (Moscow State University) did so as well in 1935 but not published until 1941)
- In 1937 George (Bell Labs) Stibitz builds a device that can represent binary numbers 0 and 1 - with Bell funding becomes the Stibitz relay machine and in 1938 the Model 1
- In 1940, Stibitz can do remote input and output via telephone lines
- 1938, William Hewlett and David Packard found HP
- 1939, John Vincent Atansoff and Clifford Berry develop a prototype computer called ABC, though contended with lawsuits may have been the first digital computer
- 1939, IBM creates the Harvard Mark 1 computer or "automatic sequence controlled calculator"

### Small Mathematical Applications
- Small programs written using machine language or form of keyboard entry, primarily scientific aploications
- Allegedly no information on application sizes, productivity rates, or quality
- 1939, British Navy installs an analog computer on HMS King George V
- Pioneers: Turing, Shannon, Zuse, Atanasoff, Aikin, Stibitz, etc.


## Chapter 3: 1940 to 1949: Computing During Wolrd War II and the Postware Era
- First Use of computer in warfare, "encryption, decryption, logistics, ballistics, and other military purposes"
- When the decade started "computer" was a job, and at the end it was a device

### Global Conflict and Computing
- All major players in WW2 had some research programs: "Australia, China, France, Germany, Great Britain, India, Italy, Japan, the Netherlands, Norway, Poland, Russia, and the United States"
- Analog was more popular than digital because they were used for ballistics
- Human computers were almost entirely women (allegedly due to lower pay)
- 1945, ENIAC first programmed by women. when device moved to military base, women lose their jobs due to gender restrictions
- 1935, IBM chairman declares equal pay for equal work (trying to stop gender pay gap)

### Wartime Innovations
#### Analog Computers During WW2
- Analog computers used for military problems due to being small and reliable (digital computers weren't small yet, and programming languages were not developed)

#### Computers in Germany During WW2
- 1945, Zuse designed what was probably the first programming language, "Plan Calculus"
- Z4 is made by Zuse which may have been the first successful digital computer
- Zuse used scrap parts and hire "invalids"
- Germany builds cryptanalysis machines to crack russian military codes
- Also builds analog computer to aim torpedos, like other countries
- V-1 cruise missile and V-2 rocket had analog computers but were not accurate, constructed using slave labor
- 1945, developed Lotfernrohr 7 bombsight, installed in first operational jet bomber, the Arado AR 234
- Germany built the Enigma and the military encoding system FISH, built on Gilbert Vermam of AT&T Bell Labs in 1917's work

#### Computers in Japan During WW2
- 1902, Yazu Ryoichi built a mechanical calculating machine called the "automatic abacus"
- 1906, Kawaguchi Ichitaro built a working mechanical computer powered by electricity
- 1944, Tokyo Imperial University builds electromechanical device for simultaneous equations
- 1960s, Japan's Nippon Calculating Machine Corportation, later Busicom, patents first microprocessor and enters agreement to manufacture it with Intel
- Japan develops analog computers for aiming submarines (was most effective at it)
- Also develops mechanical encryption machines, acquires Enigma machines in 1937
- Develops "Purple" that uses electrical step switches instead of rotors (which is what Enigma used)

#### Computers in Poland During WW2
- July 25, 1939, secret meeting held between Poland, France, and Britain at Pyry where cryptanalysis methods and an actual Enigma machine turned over to France and Britain by Polish mathematicians
- 1932, start to try to break codes used by German Enigma machine

#### Computers in France During WW2
- 1931 H.W. Egli-Bull is founded, later becomes Compagnie des Machines Bull, uses work made by Fredrik Rosing Bull to make punch-card tabulating machines

#### Computers in Australia During WW2
- CSIRAC Mark 1 develops a computer only tested in 1949, has a language called INTERPROGRAM that resembles BASIC
- "Mark 1 is perhaps the ninth working digital computer, after ABC, BINAC, COLOSSUS, EDSAC, ENIAC, Harvard Mark 1, MESM, and Z3"

#### Computers in Russia During WW2
- Limited cooperation between Russia and the Allies
- Had pioneers like S.A. Lebedev, I.S. Brook, B.I. Rameev, V.M.Glushkov, etc.
- 1948, Levedev in Ukraine builds the MESM (a small eelectronic counting machine) a general purpose computer

#### Computers in Great Britain During WW2
- 1939, Governemnt Code and Cipher School moved to Bletchley Park, by the time the war ended, 10,000 people worked there
- Turing develops the Bombe which was electromechanical
- T.H. Flowers devleops Colossus aimed at decrpting codes based on the FISH Lorenz coding scheme
- Code breaking program was called "Ultra"
- 1946, Turing writes "Automatic Computing Engine (ACE)", had subroutine calls which was in advance fo the EDVAC. Also created programming language called Abbreviated Computer Instructions
- ACE built in 1950. Larger version called MOSAIC made in 1952
- 1924, mechanical naval gunnery (analog computers) installed on HMS Rodney and HMS Nelson
- Analog computers made naval shooting 10x more accurate (400 shots vs 40 shots required for one hit at range of more than 3 miles)
- Bawdsey Research Station invents operational research, optimizing effectiveness of group activites
- Analog computers made antiarcraft shells 5x more accurate (20,000 shells vs 4000 shells)

#### Computers in the United States During WW2
- Analog computers used in ships, submarines, and bombers (eg: Norden bombsight)
- With the Norden bombsight, 50% of bombs dropped from 4k feet were accurate, as opposed to 20% accurate prior
- Norden (inventor of bombsight) then invented SBAE (Stabilized Bombing Approach) which linked autopilot to bombsight
- Tizard mission of 1940 to try and improve technical information transfer between Britain and the US
- Magnetron (by Britain (?)) allowed radar sets to be small enough to be in fighters and bombers
- TDC (Torpedo Data Computer) was an analog computer used on US submarines, including Mark III and Mark IV. Were so large they required their own control room and two extra specially trained crew members
- Mark 14 torpedo used a form of analog computer for its detonator
- Bureau of Naval Ordinance (Ordnance (?)) fixed these problems in 1943
- First U.S. digital computer was made to solve linear equations, called ABC at Iowa State University, was not programmable
- 1942, US Navy and Air Force create "research groups to apply mathematical methods to the analysis of combat operations"
- 1943 (funding) 1946 (finished), ENIAC digital comptuer to calculate artillery ballistics
- 1944, Army's Ballistic Research Laboratory funds EDVAC, finished in 1949, same purpose/people as ENIAC
- "First Draft of a Report on the EDVAC" establishes the von Neumann architecture (likely based on Turing's work)
- Harvard Mark I started during WW2 (IBM Automatic Sequence Controlled Calculator (ASCC)) electromechanical computer finished in 1944 through US Navy's Bureau of Ships (Had later Aiken designs called Mark II, III, IV, not to be confused with British Mark I)
- Grace Hopper develops COBOL
- Because Aiken tries to take sole credit for Mark I, IBM builds IBM Selective Sequence Electronic Calculator (SSEC) (last electromechanical computer) (more of a high speed calculator than a computer)

### Computers in the Postwar Era

- 1951, Institute of Radio Engineers had a committee on electronic computers, AIEE and IRE would merge to form IEEE
- Also British Computer Society, Computer Society of India, Australian Computer Society, etc.
- 1947, Goldsmith and Mann file patent for cathode ray tube as gaming device

#### Postwar Computer Development

- Manchester Automatic Digital Machine (MADM) opeartional in 1949 (britain), triggers debate on "whether computers could ever be creative"
- Williams-Kilburn tubes allow permitted random access, replaced by magnetic core memory devices in 1950s
- Retrained garment workers in Europe and US to work on computer core memories
- IBM invents DRAM
- EDSAC (Electronic Delay Storage Automatic Calculator) created in Britain
- Wheeler in Britain gets first Ph.D. in Computer Science and invents subroutines, EDSAC library has 87 subroutines
- MIT invents Whirlwind computer operational in 1951
- "hand soldering of connections [were] not easy to build ... computer industry would be a small niche industry without the later development of integrated circuits in the next decade"

- Whirlwind would become basis of SAGE and SABRE (each IBM)
- "computer games started to appear almost as soon as computers themselves"
- 1949 EMCC founded, later UNIVAC, world's first entirely digital comptuer company
- Northrup Grumman commissioned EMCC to build a corporate computer, BINAC, largest test program was 50 lines long

## Chapter 4: 1950 to 1959: Starting the Ascent of Digital Computers and Software
- Media more prominently starts to talk about computers here, see p. 80/81 for details

### Military and Defense Computers in the 1950s
#### SAGE
- "marked the transition of digital computers from dealing with a narrow band of specialized problems to tackling huge and diverse problems"
- "marked a transition [from] batch computation ... to real-time computation"
- Was also capable of running at all times (previously computers required cshutdowns every few hours)
- Need for continuous operation created concept of redundancy (backup computers)
- Was a hybrid system with 24 Direction Centers with hundreds of radar receivers, each Center having an IBM AN/FSQ-7 Combat Direction Central computer
- Also had missile launch sites
- Became operational in 1958
- "Starting with SAGE and other new weapons systems, military spending became a progresssively larger part of the national budget"

#### BOMARC
- 1959, Boeing BOMARC supersonic guided missile created for US, used analog computer for guidance
- First long-range operational air-defense missile for US

### Innovators of the 1950s
- At beginning of decade, thousands of human "computers"
- Unviersites start teaching courses on comptuers and software
- Transistors and integrated circuits invented
- AT&T Bell Labs develop prototype semiconductor based on Germanium
- 1956 Nobel Prize in Physics for discovery of transistor effect
- Lillenfield in 1920s and Heil in 1930s patented concepts similar to transistors
- Siemens AG files patent for integrated circuit "that he envisioned would use transistors"
- 1952, Geoffrey Dummer talks about need for integrated circuits
- Lebedev builds vacuum tube digital comptuer called MESM
- Brook builds M-1 computer which uses semiconductor diodes
- 1951, first commercial digital computer went on market, UNIVAC I
- 1952, Truman creates NSA for national intelligence

- Japanese are first industrial companies to use concepts of quality contol
- 1958, Kilby at Texas Instruments demonstrates working integrated circuit
- 1958, word "software" is coined

## Chapter 5: 1960 to 1969: The Rise of Business Computers and Business Software
- 1960, IBM patents magnetic strips which allows creation of credit cards, ATMs, and electric door locks

### An Evolving Workforce
- "After World War II, the U.S. business environment began to evolve from manufacturing to services. This, in turn, led to a rapid increase in clerical personnel."
- Clerical workers increase by 286% after WW2
- "Computers and software did not eliminate clerical work ... but they did change the nature of the work ... rather than vanishing [clerical jobs], clerical jobs absorbed the use of computers and software"
- "Every major bank in America, every insurance company, and every stock brokerage built data centers and recruited software personnel ... the automobile industry, the oil industry, the telecommunications industry, and the aircraft industry also grew rapidly"
- "The arrival of credit cards, debit cards, and ATMs in the early 1960s would not have been possible using only manual clerical workers"

### Early Specialized Outsourcing
- First commercial database management systems, initially sequential and fixed-length field structures
- IBM informational managements system (IMS)
- 1962 Electronic Data Systems (EDS) company formed
- "created ancillary business in the areas of specialized consulting and outsource companies"

### Computer Programmers in the 1960s
#### Becoming a Programmer
- Slide rules still popular in the 1950s
- Some universities had computer science / software engineering courses, some didn't
- IBM develops Programming Aptitude Test (PAT)
- "Programming was not usually taught at universities ... private schools run by companies that buitl comptuers such as IBM, RCA, GE, ... "
- "cowboy programming"
- "computers were used for actual ... applications during the day, the time available for testing was usually late at night"
- "desk checking ... an early and manual precursor to static analysis"
- "it was necessary to set aside rooms just to hold paper and punch cards"

- "I recall several times seeing biplanes flying low over major highways and trailing banners taht said: "Programmers - call this number: 123-4567." ... I also recall seeing skywriter planes flying over public beaches and advertising for programmers with streams of smoke that produced letters more than 100 feet high."

### Professional Status for Software Engineering
- "Software engineering is not yet a certified profession with licenses, board specialties, and malpractice monitoring, as occurs with the fields of medicine and law"
- "So long as software applications are based on custom designs and hand-coded applications line by line, software cannot really be a true engineering field. Worse, it will remain one of the most labor-intensive occupations in human history."
- "A normal mix of software applications in the 500 function point size developed using Agile and midelevle languages such as Java or C# would have an average total productivity rate of about 12 function points per staff month ... Using the same kind of 500 function point sample with the same size skill mix but using 85% certified reusable materials, the predicted productivity rate would be closer to 110 function points per staff month, or 917% higher resutls using the Agile approach. Custom designs and hand-coding cannot possibly provide the economic gains that are possible with standard reusable designs and certified reusable code."
- "Agile projects average about 4.0 bugs per function point in requirements, design, code user manuals, and bad fixes. About 93% of these are removed prior to release ... Technical debt, or fixing those delivered defects downstream, woud cost roughly $150 per function point spread over the first 18 months of usage."
- "A fundamental question for the software engineering community ... is what are the enabling technologies to move software from laborious custom designs and hand-coding to reusable designs and certified reusable modules?"
- "Assuming that the application is going to be fully constructed from standard pieces, robots or some other form of automation will carry out the actual development of the application or connect the [reusable] modules."
