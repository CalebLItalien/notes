# From Airline Reservations to Honic the Hedgehog: A History of the Software Industry - Martin Campbell-Kelly
## The Software Industry
- 1952, Fortune magazine article titled "Office Robots" describing the Univac computer at the US Census Bureau
- 1966, Business Week report "Software Gap - A Growing Crisis for Computers", which "bemoaned the shortage of programmers"
- 1980, Business Week report on software business "Missing Computer Software", appeared because of Association of Data Processing Service Organizations (ADAPSO), a "trade association of the US software industry"
- 1984, Business Week report "Software: The New Driving Force" after the rise of personal computers and now software known to most people to exist
	- "No longer was software only for corporations; now it was in the shopping malls too"

### Periodization, Sectorization, and Capabilities
- "The software industry can be divided into three sectors: software contracting, corporate software products, and mass-market software products"
	- Contracting developed along mainframes in 50s with highly custom programs at great cost
	- Corporate software products after launch of IBM System/360 in 60s, "relatively inexpensive, sold in large numbers, and thus created a much broader market for lower-cost software"
		- "Typically automated common business functions ... priced between 5,000 and 100,000 ... [most] sold in the hundreds, and a few in the thousands"
	- Personal computer in 70s created opportunity for mass-market software
		- Relatively cheap, typically between $100 and $500

### Software Contractors
- 1956, RAND corpoation created Systems Development Corporation (SDC) to develop programs for SAGE
	- First of many known as L-Systems
- At the same time as above, small startups begin like Computer Usage Company (CUC) and Computer Sciences Corpration (CSC)
	- "A successful software contractor exploited the economies of scope by specializing in particular submarkets ... firms could reduce costs by reusing software from one project in the next and could develop specialized human resources" (since dominant market were primarily bid and win contract based)
	- "Specizlied domestic knowledge enabeld non-American firmst to survive against multi-national competitors."
	- "The profits on software contracting were surprisingly low, typically less than 15 percent of sales"

### Corporate Software Products
- 1965 Applied Data Research's Autoflow
- 1967 Informatics' Mark IV
- "Previously, IBM had provided programs free of charge to customers on request, as had the other computer manufacturers. This made it difficult for software entrepeneurs to establish a market"
	- The companies that survived had been anticipating needs not satisfied by computer manufacturers
	- Under pressure of an antitrust action, they started charging for software and other services
- "The business model adopted by the software products firms, often quite consciously, was that of a producer of capital goods"
	- "Selling in volume was the only way to recover the high initial development costs of a generalized software product, which were much higher than for custom software"

### Mass-Market Software Products
- "Began in the late 1970s with the establishment of hundreds of very small software firms ... Microsoft is one of the few firms from this early period to have survived"
- 1979-80, Software Arts' VisiCalc spreadsheet and MicroPro's WordStar word processor
- Recreational software products (companies like Activision and Broderbund) come to be because of rise of personal computers
- "The persoanl computer software products industry was completely disjoint from the corporate software products industry ... in number of units sold" (much more personal computers sold than corporate)
	- Marketing expenses for corporate products was extremely high, with necessity of ongoing installation and maintenance services, but for personal computers very low (could well in retail annd mall outlets)
	- Eventually personal computer companies would get their own sales forces (like Lotus and Microsoft) ... "largely direcdted toward end users"
- "So that personal computer software products could be used by many thousands of customers without any after-sale support, programs had to have intuitive interfaces and had to require no customization"

## Origins of the Software Contractor, the 1950s
- Early computer pioneers greatly underestimated difficulty of writing programs and complexity of solving real world problems
- Extremely large programs were seen as a risk only government agencies could take
- "The software industry owed its beginnings to the desire among comptuer user for commercial enterprises that would take on this burden"

### Early Sources of Software
- Early 1950s, people majorly had to write programs for themselves
	- IBM 701 came with Principles of Operation (IBM Form 24-6042-1) of 103 pages of documentation, with some utility programs
	- IBM 701 rented at 15k a month, while a programmer earned 350 a month (at most), cohorts of 30 programmers was typical

### IBM's Technical Computing Bureau
- IBM provided the most programmer support and training
- Univac, on the other hand, provided so little that GE Appliances Division in 1954 took "2 years to get a set of basic accounting applications working satisfactorily, one entire programming group having been fired in the process"
	- Quoted in book "the whole area of automated business computing, and Univac especially, had become very questionable in the eys of many businessmen"
- Since the 30s, IBM had publisehd Pointers, which described accounting applications develoepd by customers for punch card accounting machine translation
	- Thus, they were already used to helping customers set up their machines as part of their marketing but engineering firms struggled to do this
- Providing programming support fell to Technical Computing Bureau
